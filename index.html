Research Question Verdict: Partially Confirmed (Regime Split)

Can we expect / predict mechanical compression behavior during relaxation cycles regardless of the initial seed distribution (Voronoi cell shape)?

Mapping: Dataset No. (1, 5, 10, 25, 50) = 5 independent Initial Seed Distributions · Relaxation Stage (1–100) = Lloyd Relaxation Cycles. Hypothesis: Higher relaxation cycles yield smaller inter-seed deviation. Actual Result: In the linear elastic regime, relative scatter narrows moderately (CV: 23.2% → 14.5% from Cycle 5→100), yet initial seed memory still accounts for 42–44% of variance (p < 10⁻⁷). In post-buckling crushing (5–30 mm), initial seed is negligible (p > 0.23) and scatter hits its minimum at Cycle 25 (CV = 3.9–4.8%), before diverging sharply at Cycles 50–100 due to collective buckling instabilities.

Why Elastic Seed Memory Persists

Lloyd relaxation equalizes local strut lengths (narrowing CV%), but each initial seed locks into a distinct local centroidal Voronoi minimum (topological lock-in). Anchoring to Cycle 1 cuts elastic prediction error from 13–17% down to 5.8–9.4%.

Why Post-Buckling Diverges Past Cycle 25

Moderate relaxation (Cycle 25) promotes progressive local crushing that averages out seed shape (3.6–4.7% blind error). Extreme relaxation (50–100) induces imperfection-sensitive collective buckling (5_100: 409 N vs 25_100: 573 N).
